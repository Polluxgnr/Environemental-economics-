03 · The method

Remove the seasons, then compare the change with the noise

France · anomaly = month minus the same month’s 1961–1990 mean · rainfall: same steps, in % of its 1961–1990 average

4

Speaker 2 (Pollux) · 2:15–3:20. Here is the whole method on one country, France. On the left, raw monthly temperature. Almost everything you see is the seasons: they are 92 percent of the variation, so a mild January still looks colder than a cool July. To remove them, we compare each month with the same month over 1961 to 1990: a thirty-year climate normal, and the WMO's reference period. What is left is the anomaly. On the right, the yearly anomaly, summed up by two numbers. The change, delta: the last ten years against the reference, +1.74 degrees. The noise, sigma: how far an ordinary year strays from the trend, 0.51. Their ratio, 3.4, asks one question: could the last decade pass for an ordinary year? Above two, it could not. Rainfall goes through exactly the same steps, against the same reference, in percent of normal.
