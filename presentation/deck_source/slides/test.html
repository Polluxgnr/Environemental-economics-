The “climate penalty” shrinks as we remove everything that is not weather

Naive: country effects only

−0.75 pp/°C · p = 0.01

Preferred: + year effects

−0.67 pp/°C

95% CI [−1.49, +0.16] · bootstrap p = 0.12

+ country trends

−0.04 pp/°C · p = 0.92

A typical warm year: about −0.25 pp of growth, not distinguishable from zero.

756 country-years, 1961–2025 · SE clustered by country, t(11) and wild cluster bootstrap · WDI; Atlas

Speaker 2 · 6:35–7:50. We regress growth on the temperature and rainfall anomalies over all 756 country-years. The grey lines are the naive versions. Comparing each country only with itself, a warmer year costs 0.75 points, and it looks significant. But that is partly timing: the world warmed while growth slowed after the 1960s. Year effects remove that, because they compare countries within the same year and absorb oil shocks, 2009 and COVID. That is our preferred model, in red: −0.67 points per degree, with an interval from −1.5 to +0.16. With only twelve countries we check it with a wild bootstrap: p = 0.12. Add country trends and it falls to −0.04. In words: a typical warm year might cost about 0.25 points of growth, and we cannot tell that apart from zero.
