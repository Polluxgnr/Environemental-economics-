08 · Testing it

The “climate penalty” disappears once we remove everything that is not weather

Naive: country effects only

−0.75 pp/°C · p = 0.01

+ year effects

−0.67 pp/°C · p = 0.10

fast warmers also slowed most (trends: −0.57)

Preferred: + country trends

−0.04 pp/°C

95% CI [−0.76, +0.69] · bootstrap p = 0.92

No detectable effect; losses above ~0.8 pp per °C ruled out here.

756 country-years, 1961–2025 · SE clustered by country, t(11) and wild cluster bootstrap · WDI; Atlas

Augustin · 6:40–7:45. We regress growth on the temperature and rainfall anomalies: 756 country-years. The grey lines are the naive versions: comparing each country only with itself, a warmer year costs 0.75 points of growth, and it looks significant. Year effects remove shocks every country shares, like 2009. But one problem remains: the countries that warmed fastest also slowed most. The two trends correlate at −0.57, and dropping India alone moves the estimate from −0.67 to −0.38. So our preferred model, in red, also gives each country its own trend, which leaves only year-to-year weather. The effect: −0.04 points per degree, with an interval from −0.76 to +0.69, and a wild bootstrap, built for twelve clusters, gives p = 0.92. No detectable effect, and losses above about 0.8 points per degree are ruled out in our sample. [handover] So where do climate shocks bite?
