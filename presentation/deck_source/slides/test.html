08 · Testing it

The “climate penalty” disappears once we remove everything that is not weather

Naive: country effects only

−0.75 pp/°C · p = 0.01

+ year effects

−0.67 pp/°C · p = 0.10

fast warmers also slowed most (trends: −0.57)

Preferred: + country trends

−0.04 pp/°C

95% CI [−0.76, +0.69] · bootstrap p = 0.92

No detectable effect; losses above ~0.8 pp per °C ruled out here.

756 country-years, 1961–2025 · SE clustered by country, t(11) and wild cluster bootstrap · WDI; Atlas

10

Speaker 2 · 6:35–7:50. We regress growth on the temperature and rainfall anomalies over all 756 country-years. The grey lines are the naive versions: comparing each country only with itself, a warmer year costs 0.75 points, and it looks significant. Year effects compare countries within the same year, but they only remove the trend all countries share. The countries that warmed fastest also slowed most: across our twelve countries the two trends correlate at −0.57, and dropping India alone moves the estimate from −0.67 to −0.38. So our preferred model, in red, adds one trend per country, which isolates year-to-year weather. The effect is −0.04 points per degree, with an interval from −0.76 to +0.69, checked with a wild bootstrap for our twelve clusters. In words: no detectable effect, and losses larger than about 0.8 points per degree are ruled out in our sample.
