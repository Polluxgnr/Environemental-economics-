Speaker 1 · 1:15–2:15. Climate is the weather averaged over thirty years: that is the World Meteorological Organization's definition. And a country's climate is that average over its whole territory, not at one spot. Our first version took one grid point at each capital, from a weather API. When we rebuilt the data from the Copernicus Climate Atlas, which averages the ERA5 reanalysis over each country's borders, temperature barely moved: the typical gap is 0.2 degrees. Rainfall did not survive: the typical gap is nine percentage points, and Kenya even flips sign, from +11 percent at Nairobi to −13 for the country. A warm year is warm over hundreds of kilometres; rain is local. So everything from now on is a country average. [handover] Now, how do we measure whether that climate has changed?
